Litreture Survey

Effective canine care relies on precise breed identification, accurate disease detection, and personalized treatment and nutrition planning. Traditional identification methods often misclassify mixed or similar-looking breeds, which can lead to inappropriate health management. Modern approaches now use image and audio data to classify dog breeds more reliably, helping inform better medical and dietary decisions. Skin diseases, a common issue in dogs, are typically diagnosed through visual inspection. Recent advances in image-based classification and the combination of image and symptom-based analysis have significantly improved the early detection of bacterial, fungal, and allergic skin conditions. In treatment planning, structured systems that factor in breed, age, symptoms, and disease stage help provide more relevant remedies, including the integration of natural treatments. Additionally, systems that track past shop demand and supply offer practical benefits by recommending where treatments can be purchased. Nutrition also plays a vital role in managing canine health, especially for dogs with skin sensitivities. Systems that map food ingredients to allergy risks enable more targeted dietary recommendations, reducing adverse reactions and supporting recovery. Together, these innovations enhance the quality, accuracy, and accessibility of canine care.

Breed Identification
Accurate identification of dog breeds is essential for understanding their health risks, behavior patterns, and care requirements. Misidentifying a breed—especially in mixed or visually similar dogs—can lead to inappropriate treatment and dietary plans. Traditional methods, which rely on visual observation or owner knowledge, often fall short in reliability. Recent approaches incorporate image and audio data to improve classification accuracy, enabling better decision-making in veterinary care and daily management.
Skin Disease Detection
Skin diseases are one of the most common health issues in dogs, affecting their comfort, appearance, and overall well-being. Conditions like bacterial dermatosis, fungal infections, and allergic dermatitis require early detection to ensure effective treatment and avoid complications. Manual diagnosis through physical examination may miss subtle or early-stage signs. Technological advancements now support the use of image processing and symptom-based text analysis to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of skin disease detection in dogs.
Personalized Treatment & Shop Recommendation
Veterinary treatment often follows a general template, which may not be suitable for every dog. Personalized treatment plans—based on breed, age, disease severity, and symptoms—allow for more targeted and effective care. Alongside conventional medicine, the integration of natural remedies is gaining popularity. However, access to these treatments can be a challenge. By analyzing past supply and demand data, modern systems can also predict and recommend nearby shops that stock the necessary remedies, improving accessibility and continuity of care.
Nutrition Recommendation
Nutrition plays a critical role in canine health, particularly in managing skin conditions and allergic reactions. Feeding dogs inappropriate food may worsen their symptoms and delay recovery. Conventional feeding guidelines often overlook breed-specific and health-related dietary needs. With the help of structured data mapping and classification models, it is now possible to suggest personalized diet plans that avoid allergens and support skin health based on the dog’s profile, leading to more effective long-term care.

  1. Veterinary treatment tools rarely provide tailored plans that account for breed, age, disease stage, and user preferences for natural remedies. Additionally, no current systems integrate predictive shop recommendation models to guide users on where treatments are actually available based on local demand and supply history.

Nutrition Recommendation

  1. General dog diet plans often fail to consider how specific food ingredients affect dogs with skin diseases, especially across different breeds and conditions. There is a gap in food recommendation systems that dynamically match safe, beneficial foods to a dog's full health profile while avoiding allergens that could worsen symptoms.

Web Development

  1. Reliable Breed Classification for Mixed Dogs
    Accurate breed identification is essential for proper healthcare, but existing systems often misclassify mixed or similar-looking breeds due to reliance on a single data source. Without robust identification, treatment and nutrition planning may be misaligned. A more reliable system is needed that combines both image and audio inputs to enhance breed prediction accuracy.

  2. Early and Accurate Skin Disease Detection
    Timely detection of skin diseases in dogs is critical, yet current tools depend solely on images, limiting their ability to identify subtle or rare conditions. This leads to delayed or incorrect diagnoses. Combining visual and symptom-based text analysis could improve detection accuracy and enable earlier interventions.

  3. Tailored Treatment with Natural Remedy Integration and Accessibility
    Veterinary treatment systems often fail to personalize care based on breed, age, and disease severity. More importantly, they rarely suggest natural remedies, which are increasingly preferred by pet owners. Additionally, treatment plans typically ignore the availability of remedies in nearby shops. There is a need for a treatment suggestion system that not only recommends personalized plans—including natural remedies—but also predicts where those remedies can be purchased based on past shop supply trends.

  4. Personalized Diet Planning for Skin Health
    Diet plays a key role in managing skin diseases, but existing nutrition systems overlook how ingredients affect dogs with specific conditions. This can result in allergic reactions or ineffective diets. A more refined system is required to suggest personalized food options based on a dog's breed, age, weight, and skin condition.


Main Objective

The Canine Care System aims to enhance the quality of veterinary care by delivering a user-friendly platform that supports early diagnosis, personalized treatment, and informed dietary planning for dogs. The system focuses on four major areas of canine healthcare to ensure better management, especially for dogs with skin conditions. It allows users to:

  1. Accurately identify dog breeds
    Using image and audio-based input, the system classifies dog breeds, including mixed and visually similar types. This ensures that further care—whether medical or nutritional—is breed-appropriate, reducing the risk of misdiagnosis and mistreatment.

  2. Detect and classify skin diseases
    By analyzing both uploaded images and user-entered symptoms, the system identifies common skin diseases such as bacterial dermatosis, fungal infections, and allergic dermatitis. This hybrid model increases the accuracy of detection and enables faster intervention. .

  3. Provide personalized treatment recommendations with natural remedies
    The platform generates individualized treatment plans by considering the dog’s breed, age, and disease severity. It suggests both conventional treatments and natural remedies, while also recommending nearby shops based on demand-supply trends, improving treatment accessibility.

  4. Suggest optimized nutrition plans for skin health
    The system analyzes the dog’s profile and maps food ingredients to allergy risk and skin benefits. It recommends personalized diets to support recovery and long-term skin health, while avoiding foods that may trigger or worsen skin conditions.



METHODOLOGY

The proposed Canine Care System is composed of four main components. They are:

Breed Identification, Skin Disease Detection, Personalized Treatment with Shop Recommendation, and Nutrition Recommendation. The system operates through a Flask backend, where trained models are hosted and accessed via API. Users begin by uploading a dog image and optional bark audio, which are processed using CNN and RNN models to determine the breed. Supporting datasets and breed traits were enriched using web scraping from veterinary portals. For skin disease detection, users upload skin images and describe symptoms, which are analyzed through a hybrid CNN and NLP pipeline. To enhance prediction, symptom-disease mappings were scraped from academic veterinary sources and online pet health forums. Once a disease is identified, the system generates a personalized treatment plan using structured rules and suggests both conventional and natural remedies. Cure success rates and remedy options were refined using scraped case studies and treatment blogs. In parallel, a shop recommendation engine uses KNN and cosine similarity on historical inventory data to suggest where remedies can be sourced.

Lastly, the nutrition module evaluates food suitability using a classification model and provides alternative suggestions. Food-benefit mappings were compiled from veterinary nutrition databases and scraped online pet diet resources. All components are integrated through real-time APIs, delivering a unified and accessible canine health support system.
